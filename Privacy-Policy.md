## In short

Phiru has no server of its own, shows no ads and does not track you: no tracking, no analytics tools. What you record in Phiru is stored only on your iPhone. I never get to see any of it.

Data only leaves your iPhone in these cases:

- **Maps:** for map views, your iPhone loads map tiles from Apple.
- **Strava:** when you post a run to Strava yourself.
- **Sharing:** when you pass on a picture of a run yourself.
- **Apple services you set up in iOS yourself:** for example iCloud backups, syncing Apple Health via iCloud, or crash reports to app developers.

## In the app

### What Phiru stores on your iPhone

- **Runs:** date, duration, running time, longest stretch without a break, distance, route (if turned on), your feeling and your note.
- **Goals and milestones** that you create.
- **Messages in the mailbox** that someone leaves for you, and the code for it.
- **Game progress:** your seeds and what you bought at the marketplace.
- **Settings** such as name, run days and reminder time.
- **Strava connection:** the credentials of your Strava API application and the sign-in tokens, exclusively in your iPhone’s Keychain.

So that the widget can show something, Phiru keeps a small summary, such as your name, the next session and the number of your runs, in a shared storage area on your iPhone. During a run, a Live Activity shows the current phase on the Lock Screen.

Like all app data, this data can be part of your iCloud or computer backup if you have backups turned on. Apple is responsible for that.

### Location

Phiru uses your location only during a run, also in the background with the screen locked. This way voice cues arrive on time, auto-pause notices when you stand still, and Phiru can record your route if you want it to.

Route and distance are stored only if “Record route” is turned on. Otherwise Phiru keeps the location data in memory only during the run and discards it afterwards. Phiru does not pass on your location by itself. What happens with maps and when posting to Strava is described in those sections. You grant and withdraw permission in the iOS settings.

### Maps

For the maps in the journal, in the view of a run and during a run, Phiru uses Apple Maps (MapKit). For this, your iPhone loads map tiles from Apple, in the journal automatically as soon as it shows runs with a recorded route. Apple technically receives your IP address and the requested map area, that is, the area of your route. Apple receives no other data from Phiru in the process. Without a recorded route, Phiru loads no maps.

Apple is responsible for the processing at Apple; in the EU this is Apple Distribution International Limited, Ireland. [Apple’s Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/) applies.

### Apple Health

With your permission, Phiru saves your runs as workouts (duration, distance, route) in Apple Health. Phiru does not read any health data from Apple Health. Health data is not used for advertising and is not passed on to third parties, unless you post a run to Strava yourself. Whether Apple Health syncs your data via iCloud is up to your iCloud settings. You change Phiru’s permission in the Health app.

### Strava

If you connect Phiru to your Strava account and tap “Post to Strava”, your iPhone sends the run directly to your Strava account: title, description, start time, duration, distance and, if recorded, the complete route with timestamps and elevation. Please note:

- The route is not shortened, unlike on the share picture. Who can see it is up to your privacy settings on Strava, for example by hiding the start and end of your activities on the map.
- The description is prefilled with your feeling and your note. You can change or delete it before posting.

Strava processes the data as a controller in its own right. If you live in the EU, that is Strava Ireland Limited, Dublin, Ireland. Strava may also transfer data to the USA and, according to Strava, relies on standard contractual clauses for this. [Strava’s Privacy Policy](https://www.strava.com/legal/privacy) applies. I receive none of this data.

For the connection, you create your own Strava API application. When you sign in, Phiru opens Strava’s sign-in page in a protected browser window. You enter your Strava password directly with Strava there, and Phiru never sees it. You can disconnect at any time under “More”. Phiru then also revokes its access at Strava.

### Photos and sharing

At your request, Phiru adds a picture of your run to your Photos or passes it on to an app of your choice via the iOS share menu. The picture shows the title, date, times, distance or pace and, if available, your feeling and your note. The route on it is shortened by the first and last 200 meters so the picture doesn’t show where you live. The terms of the app you choose apply to passing it on. Phiru may only add pictures to your Photos and cannot view your photos.

### Notifications

Your iPhone schedules reminders locally. There is no server and no push service.

### App Store and crash reports

You download Phiru from the App Store. Apple processes data such as your Apple Account details and the download as a controller in its own right. Apple’s Privacy Policy applies. From Apple, I only receive aggregated figures about this, for example how often Phiru was downloaded, with no reference to you as a person.

If you have allowed sharing with app developers in the iOS settings under “Privacy & Security” → “Analytics & Improvements”, Apple also provides me with crash reports and usage statistics. They contain technical details such as the device type, the iOS version and the state of the app at the time of the crash, but no run data and not your name. I use them only to fix bugs. You can change this setting there at any time.

## Legal bases

- **The app’s features** such as storage on your iPhone, maps, the widget and game progress: performance of the contract for using the app (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Phiru stores on your iPhone only what is strictly necessary for the features you use (Section 25(2) No. 2 of the German TDDDG).
- **Location, Apple Health, Photos, notifications and Strava:** your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), for health data your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). You give it via the iOS prompts and by tapping yourself.
- **Crash reports and statistics:** your consent in the iOS settings (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).
- **Email:** Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR if it concerns the use of Phiru, otherwise my legitimate interest in answering your message (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
- **Website:** my legitimate interest in a secure and accessible website (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

## Storage period

- **Data on your iPhone** stays until you delete it: individual runs in the journal, everything at once under “More” → “Delete all data”, or by deleting Phiru. Workouts in Apple Health and pictures in your Photos stay there until you delete them there.
- **Strava credentials** stay until you disconnect or delete all data. If you only delete the app, they remain in the iOS Keychain for technical reasons until you install Phiru again. Phiru then removes them on first launch. So it’s best to disconnect Strava first.
- **Emails** are deleted once your request has been dealt with, unless a legal obligation requires me to keep them.
- **Crash reports and statistics** are provided by Apple for a period set by Apple. I delete downloaded crash reports once the bug is fixed.
- **Website server logs** are stored by GitHub. GitHub decides for how long.
